This is my last attempt to make my open source work sustainable.

Starlette and Uvicorn were downloaded 1.4 billion times last month. The sponsorships I get add up to $129 a month. That wouldn't even pay for my Claude subscription.

It's my fault. My sponsors page was a tip jar. I never asked companies for anything, and I never offered them anything in return.

So for 2027 I'm offering something: a week's notice before a security release. 🔐

This year I got 180 security reports, against 22 in 2025. Every one has to be read, reproduced and answered. It's the part of my work that matters most to a company running these projects in production.

If your company is a Partner, at $1,000 a month, I tell you a week ahead: the project, the severity, the affected versions, the area of the code and the date of the fix. Not the vulnerability, not how to trigger it, not the patch. Your team gets to plan the upgrade for the day the fix ships.

I hope this doesn't get misinterpreted. Security is not a business, and I'm not selling it. Every user gets the same fix on the same day, sponsor or not.

But I can't work for free.

The target is $60,000 for 2027, through GitHub Sponsors. If you're an engineer, my sponsors page has a message you can forward to whoever holds the budget.
